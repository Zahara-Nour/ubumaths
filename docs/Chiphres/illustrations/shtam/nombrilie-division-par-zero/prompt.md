```text
Draw a newspaper illustration for a satirical article: a schoolboy has just divided by zero on his calculator and the whole town is being evacuated as a precaution.

Scene: in the foreground on the left, a small schoolboy in a school smock and a beret looks sheepish and slightly proud, holding out a small pocket calculator at arm's length; from its screen escapes a strange swirling spiral of smoke that twists upward into the sky (no symbols on the screen). Two town guards in helmets are carefully wrapping a second identical calculator in a box tied with string and sealed with red wax. In the center right, the queen from the attached character image (Mère Ubu, same face, same crimson bustle dress, same style) stands calm and authoritative in front of a large bronze spiral statue, pointing the way out with her closed fan. Behind her, townspeople leave the town in an orderly line, carrying bundles, birdcages and a grandfather clock, glancing worriedly back at the spiral in the sky.

Style: 1896 Montmartre lithographic print in the manner of Théophile-Alexandre Steinlen, as a press drawing printed in an old newspaper: bold thick dark contours, flat color areas, no gradients, no 3D, flat shadow shapes only. Funny, never scary, for students aged 11 to 17.

Colors: use only the colors of the attached palette image (muted printing inks on cream paper): navy ink for the outlines, deep crimson for Mère Ubu's dress and the wax seal, marigold yellow and brown for the bronze statue and her tiara, emerald green for the guards' uniforms, grey-blue for the swirling smoke, cream parchment for the background.

Composition: horizontal 3:2 landscape format, the schoolboy and the guards on the left, Mère Ubu and the evacuation on the right, the spiral rising between them, plain cream paper background around the scene. No text, no letters, no numbers, no signature.
```

```text
Draw a newspaper illustration for a satirical article: a schoolboy has just divided by zero on his pocket calculator, the result has escaped, and the whole town is being evacuated as a precaution.

Scene: on the left, a small schoolboy in a black school smock and a beret, sheepish but curious, has just put down a small old-fashioned pocket calculator on a cobblestone; its screen shows, large and clearly readable, only the two signs "÷ 0". From the screen, a big glowing infinity sign "∞" made of light escapes and flies away up into the sky like a runaway firework, leaving a looping trail. In the center, two town guards in green uniforms and spiked helmets act like bomb-disposal men: the first one, leaning back as far as possible, very scared, picks up that single calculator at arm's length with very long fire tongs; the second one, hiding his face behind his raised arm, holds open a heavy iron safe with its door wide open, ready to lock the calculator inside. On the right, the queen from the attached character image (Mère Ubu, same face, same crimson bustle dress, same style) stands calm and authoritative, pointing the way out of town with her closed fan, while behind her the townspeople leave in an orderly line, carrying bundles, birdcages and a grandfather clock, glancing worriedly up at the flying infinity sign.

Style: 1896 Montmartre lithographic print in the manner of Théophile-Alexandre Steinlen, as a press drawing printed in an old newspaper: bold thick dark contours, flat color areas, no gradients, no 3D, flat shadow shapes only. Funny, never scary, for students aged 11 to 17.

Colors: use only the colors of the attached palette image (muted printing inks on cream paper): navy ink for the outlines and the schoolboy's smock, marigold yellow for the flying infinity sign, its trail and Mère Ubu's tiara, deep crimson for her dress, emerald green for the guards' uniforms, dark grey for the iron safe and the tongs, brown for the townspeople's clothes, cream parchment for the background.

Composition: horizontal 3:2 landscape format, the schoolboy and the calculator on the left, the guards with the tongs and the safe in the center, Mère Ubu and the evacuation on the right, the infinity sign flying across the top of the picture. The only signs allowed are "÷ 0" on the calculator screen and the flying "∞": no other text, no letters, no other numbers, no signature.
```
