```text
Draw a newspaper illustration for a satirical article: in a Victorian machine room, a gigantic steam-powered calculating machine has been printing the same message since 1898 and nobody dares to switch it off.

Scene: the machine is a towering contraption of brass cogs, pistons, pipes and pressure gauges, puffing steam; from a slot, an endless paper tape spills out in long loops across the whole floor, printed only with the same small wavy mark repeated again and again (no letters). Next to it, the dog-headed character from the attached image (Bosse-de-Nage, same face, same clothes, same style) laughs with his mouth wide open, delighted, his brass winding key in his back. On the right, three engineers in long coats sit on stools and wait, bored, holding pocket watches; cobwebs stretch between their stools and the machine, and one of them has a long white beard reaching the floor. A guard stands stiffly beside the paper tape, checking it.

Style: 1896 Montmartre lithographic print in the manner of Théophile-Alexandre Steinlen, as a press drawing printed in an old newspaper: bold thick dark contours, flat color areas, no gradients, no 3D, flat shadow shapes only. Funny, never scary, for students aged 11 to 17.

Colors: use only the colors of the attached palette image (muted printing inks on cream paper): navy ink for the outlines, brass and marigold yellow for the machine, its pipes and the winding key, deep crimson for a few gauges and valves, emerald green for the engineers' coats, grey-white for the steam, cream parchment for the background.

Composition: horizontal 3:2 landscape format, the huge machine filling the left two thirds, the waiting figures on the right. No text, no letters, no numbers, no signature.
```
