```text
Draw a newspaper illustration for a satirical article: in a courtroom, a schoolboy claims that the queen owes him an infinitely tiny missing piece of a gold coin.

Scene: a solemn 1890s courtroom with three old judges in robes and powdered wigs, sleepy, behind a high wooden bench. On the left, a small schoolboy in a school smock and a beret stands on a stool at the bar, holding up a large gold coin in one hand and peering at its edge through an enormous magnifying glass, looking for the missing piece. On the right, the queen from the attached character image (Mère Ubu, same face, same crimson bustle dress, same style) stands calm and amused, arms crossed, holding her closed fan, one eyebrow raised.

Style: 1896 Montmartre lithographic print in the manner of Théophile-Alexandre Steinlen, as a press drawing printed in an old newspaper: bold thick dark contours, flat color areas, no gradients, no 3D, flat shadow shapes only. Funny, never scary, for students aged 11 to 17.

Colors: use only the colors of the attached palette image (muted printing inks on cream paper): navy ink for the outlines, deep crimson for Mère Ubu's dress and the judges' robes, marigold yellow for the coin, her tiara and the gold trim, emerald green for the courtroom drapes, brown for the wooden benches, cream parchment for the background.

Composition: horizontal 3:2 landscape format, the boy on the left, Mère Ubu on the right, the judges in the background in the center. No text, no letters, no numbers, no signature.
```
