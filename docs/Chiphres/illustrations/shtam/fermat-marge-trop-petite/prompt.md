Draw a newspaper illustration for a satirical article: in a dusty archive cellar, the queen from the attached character image (Mère Ubu, same face, same crimson bustle dress, same style) has just found a very old, yellowed bill and is triumphantly demanding payment.

Scene: Mère Ubu stands on the right, in three-quarter view, holding up with one hand a single old sheet of paper with a broken red wax seal and a column of illegible scribbles (no readable letters or numbers); with the other hand she points a stern finger at the viewer, a sly, greedy, satisfied smile. On the left, a tall old wooden archive cabinet with its doors wide open, overflowing with rolled documents, ledgers and dust clouds; at its foot, a thick old leather-bound book lies open, its page covered in tiny scribbles right up to the edge, with a ridiculously narrow empty margin, and a goose quill beside it. A few cobwebs, a small oil lamp on a shelf giving warm light.

Style: 1896 Montmartre lithographic print in the manner of Théophile-Alexandre Steinlen, as a press drawing printed in an old newspaper: bold thick dark contours, flat color areas, no gradients, no 3D, flat shadow shapes only. Funny, never scary, for students aged 11 to 17.

Colors: use only the colors of the attached palette image (muted printing inks on cream paper): navy ink for the outlines, deep crimson for her dress and the wax seal, marigold yellow for her tiara, the lamp light and the gold trim, emerald green and brown for the cabinet and the book cover, cream parchment for the papers and the background.

Composition: horizontal 3:2 landscape format, the two halves balanced (cabinet and book on the left, Mère Ubu on the right), plain cream paper background around the scene. No text, no letters, no numbers, no signature.
