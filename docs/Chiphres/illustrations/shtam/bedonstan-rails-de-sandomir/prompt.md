# Dessin du Shtam — bedonstan-rails-de-sandomir

Pièces jointes (nouvelle conversation Nano Banana) : `nuancier-imprimerie-reference.png`.

```text
Draw a newspaper illustration for a satirical article: a railway station master walks along an endless straight railway line across a flat steppe to check whether the two parallel rails really meet at the horizon.

Scene: two perfectly straight parallel rails run from the foreground to the horizon, where they seem to meet in a single point (strong perspective). The station master, a small round man in a navy uniform and a cap, walks away from us along the track, seen from behind and a little from the side, a long measuring tape unrolled behind him, a lantern in his hand, his shoes visibly worn out. Telegraph poles line the track, getting smaller and smaller toward the horizon. In the bottom corner, the end of a small wooden station platform with a bench and a sign post with a blank board.

Style: 1896 Montmartre lithographic print in the manner of Théophile-Alexandre Steinlen, as a press drawing printed in an old newspaper: bold thick dark contours, flat color areas, no gradients, no 3D, flat shadow shapes only. Funny, never scary, for students aged 11 to 17.

Colors: use only the colors of the attached palette image (muted printing inks on cream paper): navy ink for the outlines, emerald green and marigold yellow for the steppe grass, deep crimson for the station master's cap band and the small station roof, brown for the wooden sleepers and telegraph poles, pale blue for the sky, cream parchment for the background.

Composition: horizontal 3:2 landscape format, a low horizon line, the rails starting wide at the bottom of the image and vanishing in the center of the horizon. No text, no letters, no numbers, no signature.
```
