```text
Draw a newspaper illustration for a satirical article: a young man is overjoyed because, for the first time in his life, he has used a school theorem outside the classroom, to check whether his wardrobe can be stood up without hitting the ceiling.

Scene: in a modest 1890s living room with a low ceiling, a huge tall wooden wardrobe lies flat on its back across the floor, in the middle of the room. Standing on it, a young man in shirtsleeves and braces raises a slate high above his head with both arms, triumphant and moved to tears; on the slate is chalked only a simple right triangle with a small square right-angle mark (no letters, no numbers). A folding wooden carpenter's rule and a piece of chalk lie at his feet. A dotted arc drawn from one edge of the wardrobe up to the ceiling shows the path its corner would take, touching the ceiling exactly. In the doorway on the right, two neighbours in nightcaps peer in, cautious and unconvinced.

Style: 1896 Montmartre lithographic print in the manner of Théophile-Alexandre Steinlen, as a press drawing printed in an old newspaper: bold thick dark contours, flat color areas, no gradients, no 3D, flat shadow shapes only. Funny, never scary, for students aged 11 to 17.

Colors: use only the colors of the attached palette image (muted printing inks on cream paper): navy ink for the outlines, brown for the wardrobe and the floorboards, emerald green for the wallpaper, deep crimson for the young man's braces and the neighbours' robes, marigold yellow for the oil lamp light, dark slate grey for the slate, cream parchment for the background.

Composition: horizontal 3:2 landscape format, the wardrobe lying across the width of the picture, the young man on it slightly left of center, the neighbours on the right, plain cream paper background around the scene. No text, no letters, no numbers, no signature.
```
