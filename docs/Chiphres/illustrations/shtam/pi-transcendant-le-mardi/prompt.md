# Dessin du Shtam — pi-transcendant-le-mardi

Pièces jointes (nouvelle conversation Nano Banana) : `nuancier-imprimerie-reference.png`.

```text
Draw a newspaper illustration for a satirical article: a scholar claims to have proved that the number pi is transcendent only on Tuesdays.

Scene: in the center of a cluttered old academy study, a large Greek letter π drawn as a character, with a small face, thin arms and little legs, floats a little above the floor in a mystical pose, eyes closed, surrounded by a radiant halo, as if in a trance. On the left, a scrawny bearded scholar in a long black frock coat and a skullcap stands on top of an enormous pile of manuscript pages, almost as tall as the room, pointing triumphantly at a large wall calendar where one single column of squares is circled in red (the calendar has no letters, no numbers, only empty squares). On the right, in the doorway, a plump baker in a white apron and toque holds a big round fruit tart on a tray, staring at the floating π with suspicion.

Style: 1896 Montmartre lithographic print in the manner of Théophile-Alexandre Steinlen, as a press drawing printed in an old newspaper: bold thick dark contours, flat color areas, no gradients, no 3D, flat shadow shapes only. Funny, never scary, for students aged 11 to 17.

Colors: use only the colors of the attached palette image (muted printing inks on cream paper): navy ink for the outlines and the scholar's coat, marigold yellow for the π character and its halo, deep crimson for the circle on the calendar and the tart's fruit, emerald green and brown for the bookshelves and the furniture, cream parchment for the manuscript pages and the background.

Composition: horizontal 3:2 landscape format, the scholar on his pile of pages on the left, the floating π in the center, the baker on the right, plain cream paper background around the scene. The single Greek letter π is the only symbol allowed: no other text, no letters, no numbers, no signature.
```

## v2 (essai 1 refusé : incompréhensible, rien ne dit « mardi » ; savant en caricature de juif orthodoxe à cause de « frock coat, skullcap, beard » ; format carré)

Pièce jointe (nouvelle conversation Nano Banana) : `nuancier-imprimerie-reference.png`.

```text
Draw a newspaper illustration for a satirical article, in two halves side by side inside the same scholar's study, showing that the number pi behaves completely differently on Monday and on Tuesday.

Scene, left half: on the wall, a big tear-off desk calendar whose page shows only the word "LUNDI" in large capital letters. Below it, a large Greek letter π drawn as a character, with a small bored face, thin arms and little legs, sits slumped on an ordinary wooden chair, reading a newspaper, completely ordinary, feet on the floor.

Scene, right half: on the wall, an identical tear-off calendar whose page shows only the word "MARDI" in large capital letters. Below it, the very same π character floats high above the floor in a mystical pose, eyes closed, legs crossed, surrounded by a radiant golden halo and little sparkles; the empty chair and the dropped newspaper lie beneath it.

In the middle, between the two halves, a thin clean-shaven academic with a large curled moustache, round spectacles, a university gown and a square mortarboard cap, stands on a tall wobbly pile of manuscript pages and points one finger at each half, triumphant, as if he had just discovered it.

Style: 1896 Montmartre lithographic print in the manner of Théophile-Alexandre Steinlen, as a press drawing printed in an old newspaper: bold thick dark contours, flat color areas, no gradients, no 3D, flat shadow shapes only. Funny, never scary, for students aged 11 to 17.

Colors: use only the colors of the attached palette image (muted printing inks on cream paper): navy ink for the outlines and the academic's gown, marigold yellow for both π characters and the halo, deep crimson for the calendar headers and the mortarboard tassel, emerald green and brown for the bookshelves, the chair and the furniture, cream parchment for the manuscript pages, the newspaper and the background.

Composition: horizontal 3:2 landscape format, wider than tall (not square), the two halves clearly symmetrical, the academic in the center. The only text allowed is the two words "LUNDI" and "MARDI" on the calendars and the letter π: nothing written on the newspaper, no other letters, no numbers, no signature.
```
