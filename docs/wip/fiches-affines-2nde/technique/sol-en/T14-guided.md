We replace ~f(x)~ with its expression, then isolate ~x~. In an inequality, dividing by a negative number reverses the direction of the inequality.

1. We isolate ~x~:

   $$\begin{align*}-2x+3&=-7\\-2x&=-10\\x&=5\end{align*}$$

   $S=\{5\}$. Check: ~f(5)=-10+3=-7~.

2. We isolate ~x~:

   $$\begin{align*}-2x+3&<1\\-2x&<-2\end{align*}$$

   We divide by ~-2~, which is negative: the direction is reversed.

   ~~x>1~~

   $S=\,]1\,;+\infty[$

3. We isolate ~x~:

   $$\begin{align*}-2x+3&\geqslant 5\\-2x&\geqslant 2\end{align*}$$

   We divide by ~-2~, which is negative: the direction is reversed.

   ~~x<=-1~~

   $S=\,\left]-\infty\,;-1\right]$

These results agree with the direction of variation: ~m=-2~ is negative, so ~f~ is decreasing. Since ~f(1)=1~, ~f(x)~ is less than ~1~ exactly when ~x>1~.
