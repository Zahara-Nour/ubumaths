A quantity that starts from a fixed value and increases by the same amount for each unit is modelled by an affine function ~f(x)=mx+p~: ~p~ is the starting value and ~m~ the increase per unit.

1.  a. For ~1~ km: ~4+1.5*1=5.5~, the ride costs €$5.50$.

    For ~2~ km: ~4+1.5*2=4+3=7~, the ride costs €$7$.

    b. For ~x~ km, you pay the pick-up fee plus ~x~ times €$1.50$:

    ~~f(x)=1.5x+4~~

    c. ~f(x)~ has the form ~mx+p~ with ~m=1.5~ and ~p=4~: ~f~ is an affine function.
    d. ~p=4~ is the price paid before the taxi moves (the pick-up fee, ~f(0)=4~).

    ~m=1.5~ is the price of one kilometre: each extra kilometre increases the price by €$1.50$.

2.  a. ~f(12)=1.5*12+4=18+4=22~. A ride of ~12~ km costs €$22$.
    b. Solve ~f(x)=31~:

    ~~1.5x+4=31~~

    ~~1.5x=27~~

    ~~x=27/1.5=18~~

    The taxi travelled ~18~ km. Check: ~f(18)=27+4=31~.

    c. The customer can pay for the ride if ~f(x)<=40~, that is ~1.5x+4<=40~:

    ~~1.5x<=36~~

    ~~x<=36/1.5~~

    ~~x<=24~~

    We divided by ~1.5~, a positive number, so the inequality sign does not change. The possible distances form the interval $[0\,;24]$: the customer can travel **at most ~24~ km**.
