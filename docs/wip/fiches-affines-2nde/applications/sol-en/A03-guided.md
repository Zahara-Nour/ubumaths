A quantity that decreases by the same amount for each unit is modelled by an affine function with a negative gradient: it is decreasing. To find ~x~ when ~f(x)~ is known, solve an equation.

1.  a. ~6~ litres for ~100~ km, so ~6/100=0.06~ litre for ~1~ km.
    b. After ~x~ km, the car has used ~0.06x~ litre. So the tank still holds:

    ~~f(x)=60-0.06x~~

    c. ~f(x)=-0.06x+60~ has the form ~mx+p~ with ~m=-0.06~ and ~p=60~: ~f~ is affine.

2.  a. ~m=-0.06<0~: ~f~ is decreasing. This was predictable: the further the car goes, the less fuel is left.
    b. For each kilometre travelled, the volume of fuel goes down by $0.06$ litre.
3.  The car runs out of fuel when ~f(x)=0~:

    ~~60-0.06x=0~~

    ~~0.06x=60~~

    ~~x=60/0.06=1000~~

    The car runs out of fuel after ~1000~ km. The function ~f~ models the situation on the interval $[0\,;1\,000]$.

4.  Solve ~f(x)=15~:

    ~~60-0.06x=15~~

    ~~0.06x=45~~

    ~~x=45/0.06=750~~

    The warning light comes on after ~750~ km. Check: ~f(750)=60-45=15~.
