Adding the same number to both sides, or multiplying (dividing) both sides by the same **positive** number, does not change the direction of the inequality. Multiplying or dividing both sides by a **negative** number reverses the direction of the inequality: this comes from the direction of variation of a linear function with a negative gradient. For example, $x\mapsto -2x$ is decreasing, so if ~a<b~, then ~-2a>-2b~.

1. We isolate ~x~:

   $$\begin{align*}3x-12&>0\\3x&>12\end{align*}$$

   We divide by ~3~, which is positive: the direction does not change.

   ~~x>4~~

   $S=\,]4\,;+\infty[$

2. We isolate ~x~:

   $$\begin{align*}-2x+7&\geqslant 0\\-2x&\geqslant -7\end{align*}$$

   We divide by ~-2~, which is negative: the direction is reversed.

   ~~x<=7/2~~

   $S=\,\left]-\infty\,;\dfrac{7}{2}\right]$

3. We isolate ~x~:

   $$\begin{align*}5x+1&<2x-8\\5x-2x&<-8-1\\3x&<-9\end{align*}$$

   We divide by ~3~, which is positive: the direction does not change.

   ~~x< -3~~

   $S=\,\left]-\infty\,;-3\right[$

4. We isolate ~x~:

   $$\begin{align*}4-x&\leqslant 3x+6\\-x-3x&\leqslant 6-4\\-4x&\leqslant 2\end{align*}$$

   We divide by ~-4~, which is negative: the direction is reversed.

   ~~x>=-1/2~~

   $S=\left[-\dfrac{1}{2}\,;+\infty\right[$
