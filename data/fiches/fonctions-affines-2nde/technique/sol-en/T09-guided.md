A point $M(x\,;y)$ lies on $\mathcal{D}_f$ if and only if ~y=f(x)~: we work out the image of the x-coordinate and compare it with the y-coordinate.

1.
   a. ~f(1)=-2+5=3~, equal to the y-coordinate of ~A~: $A$ lies on $\mathcal{D}_f$.
   b. ~f(-2)=4+5=9~, different from the y-coordinate ~1~: $B$ does not lie on $\mathcal{D}_f$.
   c. ~f(3/2)=-3+5=2~, equal to the y-coordinate of ~C~: $C$ lies on $\mathcal{D}_f$.
   d. ~f(10)=-20+5=-15~, different from the y-coordinate ~-14~: $D$ does not lie on $\mathcal{D}_f$.

2. $E$ lies on $\mathcal{D}_f$, so ~y=f(-4)~, where ~f(-4)=8+5=13~.

   $E(-4\,;13)$.

3. $F$ lies on $\mathcal{D}_f$, so ~f(x)=0~: we solve ~-2x+5=0~, that is ~-2x=-5~, so ~x=5/2~.

   $F\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\,;0\right)$: this is the point where $\mathcal{D}_f$ crosses the x-axis.
