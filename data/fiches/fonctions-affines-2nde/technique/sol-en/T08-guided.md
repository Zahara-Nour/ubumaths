The graph of an affine function is a straight line: two points are enough to draw it. We choose values of ~x~ that make the calculations easy, and we take ~x=0~ to read ~p~ directly.

1. ~f_1(0)=-3~ and ~f_1(2)=4-3=1~: $\mathcal{D}_1$ passes through $A(0\,;-3)$ and $B(2\,;1)$.
2. ~f_2(0)=2~ and ~f_2(3)=-1+2=1~: $\mathcal{D}_2$ passes through $C(0\,;2)$ and $D(3\,;1)$. We chose ~x=3~ so that dividing by ~3~ gives a whole number.
3. ~f_3~ is constant: $\mathcal{D}_3$ is the line parallel to the x-axis passing through $E(0\,;-1)$ and $F(2\,;-1)$.
4. ~f_4(0)=0~ and ~f_4(2)=-3~: $\mathcal{D}_4$ passes through the origin $G(0\,;0)$ and through $H(2\,;-3)$. ~f_4~ is linear: its line always passes through the origin.
