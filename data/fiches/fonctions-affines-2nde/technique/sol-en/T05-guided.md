For an affine function ~f(x)=mx+p~, the gradient ~m~ is the rate of change between two different numbers ~a~ and ~b~: $m=\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$. We then find ~p~ by writing ~f(a)=ma+p~.

1. Gradient:

   $$\begin{align*}m&=\dfrac{f(3)-f(1)}{3-1}\\&=\dfrac{9-5}{2}\\&=2\end{align*}$$

   y-intercept: ~f(1)=5~ gives ~2*1+p=5~, so ~p=3~.

   ~f(x)=2x+3~. Check: ~f(3)=6+3=9~.

2. Gradient:

   $$\begin{align*}m&=\dfrac{f(4)-f(-2)}{4-(-2)}\\&=\dfrac{-5-7}{6}\\&=-2\end{align*}$$

   y-intercept: ~f(-2)=7~ gives ~-2*(-2)+p=7~, that is ~4+p=7~, so ~p=3~.

   ~f(x)=-2x+3~. Check: ~f(4)=-8+3=-5~.

3. Gradient:

   $$\begin{align*}m&=\dfrac{f(6)-f(0)}{6-0}\\&=\dfrac{3-(-1)}{6}\\&=\dfrac{2}{3}\end{align*}$$

   y-intercept: ~p=f(0)=-1~.

   ~f(x)=2/3x-1~. Check: ~f(6)=4-1=3~.

4. Gradient:

   $$\begin{align*}m&=\dfrac{f(5)-f(2)}{5-2}\\&=\dfrac{4-4}{3}\\&=0\end{align*}$$

   y-intercept: ~f(2)=4~ gives ~0*2+p=4~, so ~p=4~.

   ~f(x)=4~: ~f~ is a constant function.
