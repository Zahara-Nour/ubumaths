The solutions of ~f(x)=g(x)~ are the x-coordinates of the points common to $\mathcal{D}_f$ and $\mathcal{D}_g$. The solutions of ~f(x)>g(x)~ are the x-coordinates of the points where $\mathcal{D}_f$ is strictly above $\mathcal{D}_g$.

1. The two lines meet at the point with coordinates $(2\,;5)$.

2. We isolate ~x~:

   $$\begin{align*}3x-1&=-x+7\\3x+x&=7+1\\4x&=8\\x&=2\end{align*}$$

   Then ~f(2)=6-1=5~ (and ~g(2)=-2+7=5~). The common point is indeed $(2\,;5)$.

3.
   a. To the left of ~x=2~, $\mathcal{D}_f$ is below $\mathcal{D}_g$; to the right, it is above. The inequality is strict, so ~2~ is excluded.

      $S=\,]2\,;+\infty[$

   b. We isolate ~x~:

      $$\begin{align*}3x-1&>-x+7\\4x&>8\end{align*}$$

      We divide by ~4~, which is positive: the direction does not change.

      ~~x>2~~

      $S=\,]2\,;+\infty[$: we find the result read on the graph again.
