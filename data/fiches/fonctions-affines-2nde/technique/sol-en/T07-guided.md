For ~f(x)=mx+p~, we have ~f(0)=p~: the line crosses the y-axis at the point $(0\,;p)$. And when ~x~ increases by ~1~, ~f(x)~ increases by ~m~: moving one square to the right, we go up by ~m~ (down if ~m~ is negative).

1. $\mathcal{D}_1$ crosses the y-axis at $(0\,;-1)$, so ~p=-1~. From $(0\,;-1)$, moving ~1~ to the right, we reach $(1\,;1)$: we have gone up by ~2~, so ~m=2~.

   ~f_1(x)=2x-1~.

2. $\mathcal{D}_2$ crosses the y-axis at $(0\,;3)$, so ~p=3~. From $(0\,;3)$, moving ~1~ to the right, we reach $(1\,;2)$: we have gone down by ~1~, so ~m=-1~.

   ~f_2(x)=-x+3~.

3. $\mathcal{D}_3$ crosses the y-axis at $(0\,;2)$, so ~p=2~. Moving ~1~ to the right does not land on a grid point, so we move ~2~ to the right and reach $(2\,;3)$: we have gone up by ~1~ for ~2~ squares, so ~m=1/2~.

   ~f_3(x)=1/2x+2~.

4. $\mathcal{D}_4$ is parallel to the x-axis: moving to the right, we go neither up nor down, so ~m=0~. It crosses the y-axis at $(0\,;-2)$, so ~p=-2~.

   ~f_4(x)=-2~: ~f_4~ is constant.
