Increasing a number by $t\,\%$ means multiplying it by $1+\dfrac{t}{100}$; decreasing it by $t\,\%$ means multiplying it by $1-\dfrac{t}{100}$. So the new price is a **linear** function of the original price: $x\mapsto kx$, where ~k~ is the multiplier.

1.
   a. The increase is ~0.2*50=10~: the new price is ~50+10=60~, that is €$60$.
   b. The price goes up by ~0.2x~, so ~f(x)=x+0.2x=1.2x~.
   c. ~f(x)=1.2x~ has the form ~mx~: ~f~ is a linear function, with multiplier ~1.2~. The new price is proportional to the original price.
2. The price goes down by ~0.3x~, so ~g(x)=x-0.3x=0.7x~. The function ~g~ is linear, with multiplier ~0.7~.
3.
   a. After the increase, the price is ~1.2x~. Then the $30\,\%$ discount is applied: it is multiplied by ~0.7~.

      ~~h(x)=0.7*1.2x=0.84x~~

   b. ~0.84=1-0.16~: the final price is ~x-0.16x~. The price went down by $16\,\%$, not $10\,\%$: the customer is wrong. The $30\,\%$ discount is taken from a price that has already been increased, which is greater than ~x~.
4. Solve ~f(x)=54~:

   ~~1.2x=54~~

   ~~x=54/1.2=45~~

   The original price was €$45$. Check: ~1.2*45=54~.
