Company B is cheaper where its line $\mathcal{D}_g$ is below $\mathcal{D}_f$, that is where ~g(x)<f(x)~; confirm by solving this inequality.

1. ~f(x)=0.2x+50~ and ~g(x)=0.3x+30~.
2. The lines meet at the point $(200\,;90)$ and $\mathcal{D}_g$ is below $\mathcal{D}_f$ to the left of this point: company B is cheaper for less than ~200~ km.

   By calculation: ~0.3x+30<0.2x+50~ gives ~0.1x<20~, so ~x<200~. On $[0\,;400]$: $S=[0\,;200[$. For ~200~ km, both companies charge €$90$.

3. For ~150~ km: ~f(150)=80~ and ~g(150)=75~; **choose company B (€$75$)**.

   For ~350~ km: ~f(350)=120~ and ~g(350)=135~; **choose company A (€$120$)**.
