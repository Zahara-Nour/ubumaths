To find out whether a function is increasing or decreasing, compare ~f(a)~ and ~f(b)~ for any two real numbers ~a<b~, by studying the sign of the difference ~f(b)-f(a)~.

1.
   a. ~f(a)=ma+p~ and ~f(b)=mb+p~, so:

      $$\begin{align*} f(b)-f(a) &= (mb+p)-(ma+p) \\ &= mb+p-ma-p \\ &= mb-ma \\ &= m(b-a) \end{align*}$$

   b. ~a<b~, so ~b-a>0~.
   c. If ~m>0~, then ~m(b-a)~ is the product of two strictly positive numbers: ~f(b)-f(a)>0~, so ~f(a)<f(b)~.

      This holds for all real numbers ~a<b~: the images are in the same order as the numbers. **~f~ is increasing on $\mathbb{R}$.**

   d. If ~m<0~, then ~m(b-a)~ is the product of a strictly negative number and a strictly positive number: ~f(b)-f(a)<0~, so ~f(a)>f(b)~.

      The images are in the reverse order of the numbers: **~f~ is decreasing on $\mathbb{R}$.**

   e. If ~m=0~, then ~f(b)-f(a)=0~, so ~f(a)=f(b)~: **~f~ is constant**, equal to ~p~.
2.
   a. By question 1) a), with ~a=3~ and ~b=5~:

      ~~f(5)-f(3)=m(5-3)=2m~~

   b. ~f(3)<f(5)~, so ~f(5)-f(3)>0~, that is ~2m>0~. Dividing by ~2~, a positive number: ~m>0~.
3. By question 1) a), the calculation of ~f(b)-f(a)~ holds for all real numbers ~a~ and ~b~. As $a\neq b$, we have $b-a\neq 0$, so we can divide by ~b-a~:

   $$\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}=\dfrac{m(b-a)}{b-a}=m$$

   The rate of change of ~f~ between ~a~ and ~b~ is ~m~, **whatever** the real numbers $a\neq b$: it is constant. This is why ~m~ is also called the rate of change of the affine function ~f~.
