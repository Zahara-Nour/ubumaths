Let ~m~ and ~p~ be two real numbers, and let ~f~ be the affine function defined on $\mathbb{R}$ by ~f(x)=mx+p~. Choose two real numbers ~a~ and ~b~ such that ~a<b~.

1. Proving the theorem from the lesson.
   a. Write down ~f(a)~ and ~f(b)~, then show that ~f(b)-f(a)=m(b-a)~.
   b. What is the sign of ~b-a~?
   c. Suppose that ~m>0~. What is the sign of ~f(b)-f(a)~? Deduce the order of ~f(a)~ and ~f(b)~, then whether ~f~ is increasing or decreasing.
   d. Answer question 1) c) again when ~m<0~.
   e. What happens when ~m=0~?
2. An application. All we know is that ~f(3)<f(5)~.
   a. Using question 1) a), express ~f(5)-f(3)~ in terms of ~m~.
   b. Deduce that ~m>0~.
3. The rate of change. Now we only assume that $a\neq b$.

   Show that $\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}=m$. What can you say about this rate of change when ~a~ and ~b~ change?
